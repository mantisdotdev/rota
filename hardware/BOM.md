# Bill of materials

Two lists for one device (PRD §7): what to buy this week for the EVT breadboard unit (§14 phase 1: Teensy 4.1, audio shield, NeoTrellis, display, encoders, in a printed shell), and the candidate parts for the custom PCB at 500–1,000 units (§14 phase 4, DVT). Unit cost at 500 units must stay at or under $90 (§3).

## How to read this file
- Every part number, price, stock figure, lifecycle status and library date below was read from the linked page on 2026-09-02. Prices and stock move daily; re-check before ordering. Anything not read from a page that day is marked **UNVERIFIED** with the reason.
- Distributor links favour DigiKey India (about four days to Bengaluru, duty and GST collected at delivery, free shipping above INR 7,000) and Indian stockists where they hold the part. Mouser timed out on every fetch this session, so no Mouser price or stock appears; LCSC and maker prices fill the second slot where DigiKey had none. The brief asked to optimise for library support and delivery to India, not price.
- Premium rule (from the brief): spend on what the player touches, hears or sees (pads, encoders and caps, codec and headphone amp, speaker, display, shell); cheapest reliable option everywhere else. Felt parts name one cheaper alternative and what it loses.
- Nothing here has run on real hardware (CLAUDE.md, Landmines). Pin assignments are not in this file; they belong to the bring-up runbook.

## Noticed while researching, left for the user

These are findings outside the BOM's scope that change the PRD or the plan. Nothing here has been changed in the repo.

- **The 20-hour headphone target (§7.7) is not reachable with the parts as specified.** 2500 mAh over 20 h is 125 mA average at the cell. PJRC's own figure is about 100 mA for the MCU alone at 600 MHz, before the backlight (60–160 mA on the panels checked), codec and LEDs. The production power research estimates 9.5–14 h on headphones and 5.5–7 h on speaker with the CPU at 600 MHz and the backlight at 30–50 %; the EVT unit, which also carries two NeoTrellis boards and a boost converter, will run about 3–5 h. Reaching 20 h needs the RT1062 clocked down (PJRC publishes no current figure for lower clocks, so it must be measured at EVT), the backlight dimmed and slept aggressively, and the pad LEDs held low. This is a PRD decision, not a part choice.
- **India's Compulsory Registration Scheme.** Importing the lithium cell needs a BIS CRS registration under IS 16046 (Part 2), tested in a BIS-recognised Indian laboratory, one registration per factory. The same CRS product list names "Electronic Musical System with input power below 200 Watts, IS 616:2017", which may cover Rota itself. §7.7's certification path lists UN38.3, IEC 62133, FCC, CE, IC and RoHS but not BIS; the §16 compliance budget should be checked against this.
- **NXP longevity for the i.MX RT1060.** Search snippets disagree: one says the family entered a 10-year programme in 2017 (ending 2027), another, an NXP community post titled "Longevity Extended to 15 Years on More than 100 MCU Devices", says the RT1060 was extended to 15 years from launch (to about 2032). Every nxp.com page returned 404 and the community post 403 to every fetch, so neither is verified on a page. Confirm on NXP's Product Longevity list before DVT; a 2027 end date would be a problem, a 2032 one would not.
- **The EVT pads are smaller than spec.** The Adafruit keypad's keys are 10 mm on a 15 mm pitch against §7.2's ~16 mm, so usability round 1 tests feel with smaller pads than the product will have; the SparkFun pad's 15 mm keys are the closer stand-in for size.
- **The codec meets §7.4 with no margin, and its common variant is end-of-life.** The SGTL5000's headphone output is 30 mW typical into 32 Ω at 3.3 V, exactly the PRD's floor, and it is the only codec with a Teensy driver that reaches it; the 1 mm-body SGTL5000XNAA3 that most reference designs and the audio shield's Rev D footprint use is end-of-life, so the custom board designs in the 20-pin XNLA3 (in stock, 39-week lead) and buys the run quantity early. If supply fails, the only driver-supported fallback (TLV320AIC3206) gives 22.5 mW, which would need §7.4 relaxed.
- **The class-D amp cannot reach §7.4's 1–2 W from the battery rail.** The PAM8302A gives about 0.65 W into 8 Ω at 1 % THD from 3.6 V and 1.3 W from 5 V; the production power design has no 5 V rail. Either a small boost for the amp or a relaxed loudness figure is a DVT decision after the pocket listening test.
- **Headphone detect is not one GPIO with this codec.** Every detect jack found puts its switch on the tip conductor, and the SGTL5000 drives headphones against a virtual ground that must not meet system ground, so an unplugged tip idles at mid-rail. The detect needs an ADC pin or a level-shifted pull-up, and the headphone jack sleeve must be isolated from the other jacks and from any metal panel. This shapes the schematic and the enclosure's port strip.
- **Teensy supply has moved.** PJRC stopped selling boards in March 2025; SparkFun manufactures and sells Teensy, Adafruit has dropped it, and DigiKey India has zero plain Teensy 4.1 boards with a 12-week lead while the with-headers and lockable variants are in stock. The bootloader chip for the custom board is still sold only by PJRC.
- **Bootloader v1.09 changed the flash rules.** Chips shipped before April 2026 refuse Winbond "-Q" flash parts; the "-M" ordering code works with every version. Any DIY Teensy 4.1 board built for DVT must match its bootloader version to its flash.
- **60 fps on a 2.8" SPI panel is a partial-redraw problem, not a panel choice.** A full 320 × 240 × 16-bit frame is 1.23 Mbit; at the datasheet SPI ceilings (ILI9341 100 ns write cycle, ST7789V 66 ns) that is about 8 and 12 full frames per second. Every Teensy driver runs SPI far above spec, and ILI9341_T4 reports about 45 full frames at 60 MHz, so §7.3's 60 fps ring depends on redrawing only the ring region (which ILI9341_T4's differential engine does) or on the HAL doing partial-window updates itself. This belongs in the `ui/` design before the panel is frozen.
- **The §3 unit-cost ceiling is at risk.** The priced production picks alone sum to about USD 73 of the USD 90 ceiling at 500 units, before the battery cell, the silicone keypad and its tooling, the PCB, assembly, the enclosure and packaging. The bootloader chip (USD 6.25), the MCU (USD 9.36) and the display (USD 18 or more) are the three largest lines; the display and the pads are the premium parts the brief protects, so the ceiling, the volume or the bootloader decision (D-058) is where the slack has to come from.
